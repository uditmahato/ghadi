# References for the paper

Checked on 14 October 2026 against publisher pages, institutional repositories or
abstracts. "Confirmed" means the authors, title, journal, volume and pages or DOI were
found in at least one independent record. Read each paper before citing it for a
specific claim; this list only confirms that the reference is real and correct.

## Closest work

1. **Cook, K. L., Rekapalli, R., Dietze, M., Pilz, M., Cesca, S., Rao, N. P., Srinagesh,
   D., Paul, H., Metz, M., Mandal, P., Suresh, G., Cotton, F., Tiwari, V. M., and
   Hovius, N. (2021).** Detection and potential early warning of catastrophic flow
   events with regional seismic networks. *Science* 374(6563), 87 to 92.
   doi:10.1126/science.abj1227. **Confirmed.**
   Chamoli 2021 on a regional network in Uttarakhand: all phases detectable from up to
   100 km, warning within minutes, up to 11 minutes for the worst hit site. The authors
   say real time use needs automated detection that does not yet exist. This paper's
   contribution is that system, run live, with its false alarms measured.

2. **Cook, K. L., Andermann, C., Gimbert, F., Adhikari, B. R., and Hovius, N. (2018).**
   Glacial lake outburst floods as drivers of fluvial erosion in the Himalaya.
   *Science* 362(6410), 53 to 57. **Confirmed** (title, authors, journal; check the page
   numbers on the journal page).
   The 5 July 2016 outburst flood in the same Bhote Koshi valley, recorded by
   seismometers installed after the Gorkha earthquake.

## The events

3. **Shugar, D. H., and 50 others (2021).** A massive rock and ice avalanche caused the
   2021 disaster at Chamoli, Indian Himalaya. *Science* 373(6552), 300 to 306.
   doi:10.1126/science.abh4455. **Confirmed.**
   About 27 million cubic metres of rock and ice from Ronti Peak; over 200 dead or
   missing.

4. **Walter, F., Amann, F., Kos, A., and others (2020).** Direct observations of a three
   million cubic meter rock slope collapse with almost immediate initiation of ensuing
   debris flows. *Geomorphology* 351, 106933. doi:10.1016/j.geomorph.2019.106933.
   **Confirmed.** Earlier drafts of the paper plan put this in *Geophysical Research
   Letters*; that was wrong.
   Bondo 2017 (exp031): the first debris flow began 30 s after the collapse.

5. **Higman, B., Shugar, D. H., Stark, C. P., and others (2018).** The 2015 landslide and
   tsunami in Taan Fiord, Alaska. *Scientific Reports* 8, 12993.
   doi:10.1038/s41598-018-30475-w. **Confirmed.**
   Taan Fiord 2015 (exp033): found from its seismic signal; tsunami runup up to 193 m.

6. **Sattar, A., and others (2025).** The Sikkim flood of October 2023: drivers, causes,
   and impacts of a multihazard cascade. *Science*. doi:10.1126/science.ads2659.
   **Confirmed** (title, journal, DOI; check volume and pages).
   About 14.7 million cubic metres of moraine into South Lhonak Lake, about 50 million
   cubic metres of water released.

7. **Melamchi 2021.** No single standard reference was confirmed. Candidates: Duwadi and
   others (2024), Assessment of the 2021 debris flow and flood related loss and damage
   in Melamchi Watershed of Central Nepal, *Journal of Nepal Hydrogeological
   Association*, doi:10.3126/jnha.v1i1.78226 (confirmed record); and the multiscale
   cascading hazard and erosional cascade studies by Zekkos, Clark and others (journal
   not yet confirmed). **To finish.**

## Seismic methods

8. **Ekström, G., and Stark, C. P. (2013).** Simple scaling of catastrophic landslide
   dynamics. *Science* 339(6126), 1416 to 1419. doi:10.1126/science.1232887.
   **Confirmed.**

9. **Hibert, C., Ekström, G., and Stark, C. P. (2014).** Dynamics of the Bingham Canyon
   Mine landslides from seismic signal analysis. *Geophysical Research Letters* 41(13).
   doi:10.1002/2014GL060592. **Confirmed.**

10. **Chmiel, M., Walter, F., Wenner, M., Zhang, Z., McArdell, B. W., and Hibert, C.
    (2021).** Machine learning improves debris flow warning. *Geophysical Research
    Letters* 48(3). **Confirmed** (volume and issue from the ETH record; add the article
    number from the journal page).
    Illgraben, Switzerland: all 13 confirmed 2020 events alarmed with no false alarms, on
    a dense local network. The contrast with two open stations at 56 and 131 km is the
    point.

11. **Burtin, A., Bollinger, L., Vergne, J., Cattin, R., and Nábělek, J. L. (2008).**
    Spectral analysis of seismic noise induced by rivers: a new tool to monitor
    spatiotemporal changes in stream hydrodynamics. *Journal of Geophysical Research*
    113, B05301. doi:10.1029/2007JB005034. **Confirmed.**
    River noise on the Trishuli above 1 Hz rose by up to 20 dB in the 2003 monsoon:
    background for the noise these stations hear.

12. **Allen, R. V. (1978).** Automatic earthquake recognition and timing from single
    traces. *Bulletin of the Seismological Society of America* 68(5), 1521 to 1532.
    Standard reference for the STA/LTA trigger.

13. **Nakamura, Y. (1989).** A method for dynamic characteristics estimation of
    subsurface using microtremor on the ground surface. *Quarterly Report of the Railway
    Technical Research Institute* 30(1), 25 to 33. Standard reference for the H/V ratio.
