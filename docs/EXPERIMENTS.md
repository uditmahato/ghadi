# Experiment index

Experiments are immutable once run. A new question gets a new numbered directory —
never an edit to an old one. Negative results are committed, not deleted.

| ID | Question | Status | Headline finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| [exp001_signal_recon](../experiments/exp001_signal_recon/FINDINGS.md) | Is the 26 Aug 2026 mass-movement signal present and separable on NK.KKN? | **Reproduced 2026-09-02** | Signal present and cleanly triggered. Both spectral orderings reproduce (cascade highest LF/HF, lowest centroid); absolute values differ ~3× from the original, traced to the unpublished low/high split frequency. The settling artefact is worse than documented — it survives the prescribed LTA-length guard when the taper is length-proportional. |
| [exp002_feature_rework](../experiments/exp002_feature_rework/FINDINGS.md) | Does the M2 feature rework survive contact with real data? | **Run 2026-09-02** | Mixed, and the negative half matters more. The signal-presence gate (2.3) works and fixes exp001's headline failure; response deconvolution (2.4) makes units physical without changing discrimination. **`emergence_s` still fails**: on a regional earthquake it measures the S−P interval rather than the source ramp, so it is contaminated by distance. Do not put it in the M3 classifier, and do not tune it on two events. |
| [exp003_corpus_separation](../experiments/exp003_corpus_separation/FINDINGS.md) | Does the spectral separation survive a real earthquake corpus (n=64)? | **Run 2026-09-02** (carries an appended correction) | **The clean-separation claim is retired.** 12.5% of real earthquakes look at least as mass-movement-like as the cascade on both features at once; against magnitude- and distance-matched events that falls to 1 in 25. Magnitude, not distance, is the confound (rank r ±0.4). A third of corpus windows contained a pre-origin trigger. **Its availability claim was over-read and is withdrawn — see the correction at the top.** |
| [exp004_false_alarm_rate](../experiments/exp004_false_alarm_rate/FINDINGS.md) | What false-alarm rate does the detector run at against 226 h of noise? | **Run 2026-09-02** | **12.9 per station-month against a target of ≤1.** The spectral criteria do most of the work (30× reduction from the STA/LTA baseline's 387), and the signal-presence gate removes nothing. **Two of the four false alarms are confirmed teleseisms** — distant earthquakes look low-frequency because attenuation strips the highs, so they are not separable on these features and must be suppressed by global-catalogue cross-check instead. POD is not estimable at n=1: this is half an ROC. |
| [exp005_window_length](../experiments/exp005_window_length/FINDINGS.md) | Is the separation an artefact of measuring over a 35-minute window? | **Run 2026-09-02** | No, but exp003 was mildly optimistic. Background is high-frequency (centroid 5.03 Hz) and does dilute short events, yet energy dominates duration so the effect is small. Measured like-for-like on 120 s segments the overlap is **17.2%, not 12.5%** — and the segment figure is the only operationally realisable one, since a 35-minute feature is unavailable at decision time. |
| [exp006_teleseism_suppression](../experiments/exp006_teleseism_suppression/FINDINGS.md) | How much does suppressing distant earthquakes buy? | **Run 2026-09-02** | **False-alarm rate halves, 12.9 → 6.5 per station-month.** Getting there required fixing the rule: a tolerance around **P** caught only one of two, because STA/LTA fires on the largest arrival, which for a distant event is S/Lg or the surface train — one trigger landed 647 s after P and 24 s after Lg. Suppression is a **P-to-surface phase window**, not a tolerance. Over-suppression is 8.3% of triggering windows, and the cascade itself survives (asserted in tests). |
| [exp007_station_coverage](../experiments/exp007_station_coverage/FINDINGS.md) | Can the positive class grow, and on which station? | **Run 2026-09-02** | **Yes — on IO.EVN, which holds all six candidate events including Gorkha 2015 and Jure 2014 that NK.KKN cannot reach.** Union coverage is 119 months against NK.KKN's 71, and the two stations' gaps are largely independent. R1 is less severe than it looked, and IO.EVN is the deeper archive rather than mere redundancy — though a false-alarm rate is per station and features are not interchangeable across them. |
| [exp008_candidate_positives](../experiments/exp008_candidate_positives/FINDINGS.md) | Can the positive class grow beyond n=1? | **Run 2026-09-02** | **No — n=1 stands.** The value is in what it rejected: a "strong" Rasuwagadhi candidate had the *nearer* station firing 17 s **later**, which no single source can produce. Requiring the lag to match the source location in sign as well as size rejects it. Structurally, cross-station corroboration is unavailable for 3 of 5 events, and fails hardest for small ones — Thame gave 9 triggers at 21 km and **zero** at 132 km. |
| [exp009_corrected_noise_corpus](../experiments/exp009_corrected_noise_corpus/FINDINGS.md) | What changes when the noise corpus stops containing earthquakes? | **Run 2026-09-03** | **27 of 388 windows were not noise** — teleseisms from Japan, Argentina, Mexico, Papua New Guinea, Russia. Corpus now 366 usable, rate 6.8 per station-month. **The rate did not improve**: 6.5 and 6.8 are the same two events and the denominator moved. What improved is that the corpus is correctly labelled. Consistency check passes exactly — runtime suppression finds *zero* further teleseisms in the corrected corpus. |
| [exp010_second_station](../experiments/exp010_second_station/FINDINGS.md) | Does the spectral separation reproduce on a second station? | **Run 2026-09-06** | **Yes, and more strongly — the separation is source physics, not an NK.KKN site effect.** On IO.EVN (131 km, independent site) the cascade sits even further into the low-frequency tail: LF/HF 15.07 vs earthquake median 2.22, overlap 4.5% (6/132) vs NK.KKN's 17.2%. Magnitude confound persists but weaker (+0.26/-0.31); distance confound now visible (IO.EVN has real near-field coverage). Same physical event, so the positive class is still n=1 — this corroborates the feature across stations, not the population. |
| [exp011_lead_time_headline](../experiments/exp011_lead_time_headline/FINDINGS.md) | How many minutes of warning would the 2026 event have delivered to each settlement? | **Run 2026-09-07** (offline) | **Bidur ~36–37 min, Syabrubesi ~9–10, Timure 2–3 — and pipeline latency barely moves it.** Lead time is set by the water's travel, not detection speed, so a faster pipeline is not where the warning comes from. Bidur is winnable and reality agrees (it evacuated on ~14 min notice, inside the model's 18 min at a 20-min alert). **Timure is not a latency problem** — at ~4 min surge arrival it is below the actionable floor in every scenario including 30 s. n=1. |
| [exp012_satellite_confirmation](../experiments/exp012_satellite_confirmation/FINDINGS.md) | Can radar imagery confirm the 2026 source, and find the other events? | **Run 2026-09-10** (Sentinel-1, Planetary Computer) | **The 2026 source is confirmed independently of the seismic record.** On two of three tracks the event pair's changed patch is 3.3x and 4.0x its own pre-event control (floor 2.0x, fixed beforehand); the third is 1.85x. All three tracks put their largest patch in one cluster about 3 km across, 4 to 6 km north of the catalogued point, inside its 8 km uncertainty. **No other candidate is cleanly confirmed**: snowmelt and monsoon controls of 1 to 13 km2 swamp Langtang, Melamchi, and Rasuwagadhi; Thame shows two tracks agreeing to 0.3 km with one marginally above background, a lead not a label. Jure predates the archive. Every pair in every season shows raw "change", so controls are indispensable. Imagery adds no lead time. n=1 stands. |
| [exp013_cross_track_excess](../experiments/exp013_cross_track_excess/FINDINGS.md) | Do independent radar tracks agree on WHERE the ground changed, and does that beat a no-event null? | **Run 2026-09-14** (offline, from exp012's cache) | **Yes for 2026: a 0.41 km2 patch where at least two of three tracks see change in the event window and not in their own controls, 5.5x the largest patch the same test finds for the pre-event window on the same ground.** Three-track agreement is 6.6x its null. Thame 2024 gives 2.4x on a 0.05 km2 patch, a modest lead. Langtang, Melamchi, and Rasuwagadhi fall below their nulls (0.05x to 0.45x): snowmelt and monsoon change is everywhere and nowhere in particular. This pixel-level test replaces exp012's coarse largest-patch statistic and needs no network. n=1 stands. |
| [exp014_null_distribution_vh](../experiments/exp014_null_distribution_vh/FINDINGS.md) | Is the 2026 radar signal extreme against many no-event windows, and does VH see it too? | **Run 2026-09-17** (Sentinel-1 VV and VH) | **Partly.** With all three tracks the event's agreement patch is the largest of every clean like-for-like null window, in both polarisations (VV 14.9x and VH 4.5x the largest null), and VV and VH independently place it 0.11 km apart. But only three three-track nulls exist, so the best possible rank p = 0.25 is what it gets: extreme among few, not significant. On two tracks (85 + 121) the event sits inside four May and June nulls (VV p 0.6, VH p 0.8). The signal needs all three geometries. n=1 stands. |
| [exp015_seasonal_nulls_2026](../experiments/exp015_seasonal_nulls_2026/FINDINGS.md) | Is the 2026 radar signal extreme against the same late August weeks in 2022 to 2025? | **Run 2026-09-17** | **Yes on three tracks, no on two.** Against 13 like-for-like three-track nulls, the 2026 VV patch (0.413 km2) is 10.5 times the largest null (0.040) and VH (0.115) is 2.6 times (0.045); both rank first at the new floor p = 0.071, and VV and VH agree on location within 0.1 km. On tracks 19 and 85 alone a 2023 null (after 2 to 6 Sep) is larger in both polarisations; a spot about 2 km from the source near the river also changes in 2022, likely seasonal. Nulls share images, so the effective count is below 13. |
| [exp016_thame_null_test](../experiments/exp016_thame_null_test/FINDINGS.md) | Does the Thame 2024 radar lead survive a proper null test? | **Run 2026-09-17** | **No.** On all three tracks Thame ranks second of nine in both polarisations (VV 0.051 vs 0.115 km2, VH 0.026 vs 0.114, p = 0.22); the larger null is peak monsoon 2022. On two tracks it ranks first only at the floor (0.20 to 0.25). In 7 of 30 null comparisons the largest patch also falls within 0.7 km of the spot exp012 flagged, so that spot changes in ordinary years. The pre-run claim that 2021 would give no nulls was wrong. Positive class stays at one event; Thame may be a kind of event this test cannot see. |
| [exp017_fusion_warning_gate](../experiments/exp017_fusion_warning_gate/FINDINGS.md) | Where do the two WARNING gate rules disagree? (#26) | **Run 2026-09-17** (offline) | **In one situation only, and there the historical rule breaks monotonicity.** Across 64 sensor states the rules differ in 7, all the same case: a gauge detects while the seismic station is online and quiet (historical WARNING at score 0.81, strict ADVISORY). Adding a live, quiet sensor raised the tier in 14 states under the historical rule and 0 under the strict rule. The 2026 event is WARNING under both. Strict rule implemented behind a flag, default unchanged pending the owner's decision. Also found: four dashboard presets silently ran with the seismic station switched off. |
| [exp018_io_evn_false_alarm_rate](../experiments/exp018_io_evn_false_alarm_rate/FINDINGS.md) | What false alarm rate does the detector run at on IO.EVN, and on NK.KKN at decision time? (#3) | **Run 2026-09-17** | **Far above target at both stations.** On the decision time basis (120 s after each trigger) IO.EVN gives 29 false alarm windows in 238.6 h, 88.8 per station month (95% CI 59.5 to 127.5); 372 of 409 noise windows trigger. NK.KKN on the same basis gives 13.7 (3.7 to 35.0), about twice the whole window 6.8 of exp009, which was reproduced exactly. IO.EVN alarms span 11 months and all times of day, with LF/HF up to 463, and may include real ice and rock falls that did not become floods. Thresholds are fitted to n = 1, so these are lower bounds. |

## Standing consequences for the codebase

Things later work must not undo, each traceable to an experiment:

| Consequence | Where enforced | From |
|---|---|---|
| No hand-weighted composite score | `tests/test_features.py` | exp001 Finding 4 |
| `emergence_s` measured against the global peak is deprecated, not the M2 fix | `ghadi.features` | exp001 Finding 4 |
| Settling guard covers **taper + LTA**, not LTA alone | `ghadi.detect.sta_lta` | exp001 Finding 5 |
| The preprocessing taper is a fixed duration, never a fraction of the window | `ghadi.features.preprocess`, `tests/test_sta_lta.py` | exp001 Finding 5 |
| An LF/HF ratio is meaningless without its split frequency | to be honoured by issue 2.6 | exp001 Finding 2 |
| Onset time is threshold-dependent for an emergent source; publish the threshold with any lead-time figure | to be honoured by M5 | exp001 Finding 3 |
| Emergence needs a signal-presence gate as well as an onset — 2.1 and 2.3 are one issue | `ghadi.features`, `tests/test_features.py` | exp002 |
| The emergence search is bounded, which keeps it causal and stops it spanning two arrivals | `ghadi.features`, `tests/test_features.py` | exp002 |
| `emergence_s` is excluded from the M3 classifier until distance contamination is resolved | to be honoured by M3 | exp002 |
| "First trigger in the window" is not a safe onset pick — a window cut on a catalogue time can contain an earlier unrelated event | `ghadi.detect.onset`, `scripts/harvest_earthquakes.py` | exp002, confirmed at scale by exp003 (a third of windows) |
| Never describe the spectral separation as "clean" — 12.5% of real earthquakes meet the cascade's own values on both features | to be honoured by every paper and README | exp003 |
| A classifier over these features must control for magnitude **and distance**, or it learns a size/distance detector rather than a mass-movement detector | to be honoured by M3 | exp003, distance confirmed by exp010 |
| Report the assumed operating point's margin: a threshold at the cascade's own value is fitted to one event and is a lower bound, not an estimate | `ghadi.classify` (carried in every classification's reason) | exp003 |
| The seismic decision rule is a **conjunction of per-feature thresholds, never a hand-weighted composite score** | `ghadi.classify`, `tests/test_classify.py` | exp001 Finding 4 |
| Train and evaluate on features computed over a **decision-time segment**, never a window extending past the moment the alert must fire — otherwise train/serve skew with a safety cost | to be honoured by M3 | exp005 |
| Quote 17.2% as the earthquake overlap, not 12.5% | to be honoured by every paper and README | exp005 |
| Teleseism suppression by global-catalogue cross-check is an architectural component, not a later filter — distant earthquakes are not separable from mass movements on the spectral features | `ghadi.teleseism` | exp004, implemented after exp006 |
| Suppression spans a **P-to-surface phase window**, never a tolerance around P — STA/LTA fires on the largest arrival, which for a distant event is S/Lg or the surface train | `ghadi.teleseism`, `tests/test_teleseism.py` | exp006 |
| Any suppressor must be asserted not to remove the 26 Aug 2026 cascade — the worst and quietest failure is one that improves every false-alarm metric by eating the target | `tests/test_teleseism.py` | exp006 |
| Extend the noise-corpus exclusion catalogue to global M≥5.5 with travel-time-aware windows, not M≥3.5 within 6° | `scripts/harvest_noise.py` | exp004, done in exp009 |
| Corpus exclusion and runtime suppression must share **one** `phase_window` definition — if they drift, the measured false-alarm rate describes a system nobody runs | `ghadi.teleseism`, `tests/test_teleseism.py` | exp009 |
| Never quote a false-alarm rate as detector performance while POD is unestimable at n=1 | to be honoured by every paper | exp004 |
| A false-alarm rate is **per station** — NK.KKN's 6.5/station-month says nothing about IO.EVN, and the two must never be pooled | to be honoured by M3/M5 | exp007 |
| Corpora should be drawn across **both** stations: the union is 119 months against NK.KKN's 71, and their gaps are largely independent | to be honoured by issues 1.2/1.3 | exp007 |
| Cross-station agreement requires the lag to match the source location **in sign as well as size** — "close in time" is satisfied by any two unrelated transients | `experiments/exp008_candidate_positives` | exp008 |
| Station agreement is structurally blind to small events, which are single-station by nature — the independent **gauge** channel is the only corroboration available at the small end, making B2 more central than the risk table implies | to be honoured by M4 | exp008 |
| Radar before/after pairs are only ever formed on the **same relative orbit and pass direction**; comparing different tracks manufactures change from viewing angle alone | `ghadi.eo_fetch.same_track_pairs`, `tests/test_eo_fetch.py` | exp012 |
| One frame per satellite pass (the best covering one), and a control comes from the **previous acquisition cycle**, never another frame of the same pass, which is near-identical and would fake a quiet control | `ghadi.eo_fetch.one_per_pass`, `previous_pass`, `tests/test_eo_passes.py` | exp012 |
| A raw radar change threshold is not a verdict: every pair in every season shows "change". An event pair counts only if its largest patch is at least 2.0x a same-track pre-event control, a ratio fixed before the 2026 pairs were seen | `ghadi.eo.compare_to_control`, `tests/test_eo_control.py` | exp012 |
| When too little of a region is usable the verdict is **inconclusive**, never "no change": a scene nobody could see is not a scene where nothing happened | `ghadi.eo`, `tests/test_eo.py` | exp012 |
| Satellite imagery is analysis path only and adds no lead time; it confirms WHERE and bounds WHEN to the interval between passes, and must never be presented as a warning channel | `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, `ghadi.eo` docstring, dashboard satellite panel | exp012 |
| A single-track "changed patch" is not a localized result. The localized test is pixel-level agreement across independent tracks, each net of its own control, judged against the same test on a pre-event window of the same ground | `ghadi.eo.cross_track_excess`, `ghadi.eo_fetch.align_arrays`, `tests/test_eo_cross.py` | exp013 |
| Null windows must match the event window's cadence (11 to 13 day gaps; 7 day windows from a second satellite are skipped) and its track set (a two-track null is compared only with the event on the same two tracks). A rank from a handful of nulls is reported with its floor, never as significance | `ghadi.eo_fetch.cycle_windows`, `tests/test_eo_cycles.py` | exp014 |
| Adding a live channel that detected nothing must never raise the alert tier; a gate that counts evidence groups must be able to tell reporting from detecting | `ghadi.fusion` (`supporting_groups`, `warning_requires_supporting_groups`), `tests/test_fusion_gate.py` | exp017 |
| A false alarm rate is quoted on the basis the live detector decides on (the segment after each trigger), per station, with its interval | `scripts/harvest_noise.py --station`, `experiments/exp018_io_evn_false_alarm_rate` | exp018 |
| A spot where tracks agree is only evidence if it does not also change in the same weeks of other years | `ghadi.eo_null.seasonal_window_sets`, `compare_event_to_nulls` | exp015, exp016 |
