# Letter — DHM

**To:** Director General, Department of Hydrology and Meteorology
**From:** [SIGNATORY, INSTITUTION]
**Subject:** River-gauge data at native sampling, and the status of CAP adoption
**Status:** DRAFT — not sent

---

Dear [NAME],

We are developing a detection system for cascading flood surges in transboundary
Himalayan rivers, prompted by the 26 August 2026 Bhote Koshi event. It listens to
continuous seismic waveforms for the signature of a large mass movement upstream and
corroborates that against downstream gauge behaviour. It detects; it does not forecast.

We are writing about two things.

**First, river-gauge series at native sampling rate.** Our second detection channel
looks for an anomalous *rate of rise* rather than a crossing of an absolute stage
threshold. This distinction is the point: on 26 August the Trishuli rose roughly nine
metres in thirty minutes, and four of the five downstream stations were destroyed before
their absolute thresholds were reached. A gauge that is watched for how *fast* it is
rising can report a surge in the minutes before it is overwhelmed, using instruments
that are already installed.

For this we need stage readings at whatever interval the telemetry actually records,
rather than the aggregated values published on River Watch. Historical series for the
Bhote Koshi–Trishuli corridor would let us develop and validate the method; we are not
asking for a real-time feed at this stage.

We recognise this needs a data-sharing agreement and we are glad to work through
whatever form DHM requires. We would also note that our design deliberately does not
depend on it: the seismic detector works alone, and gauge data improves it rather than
enabling it. We would rather describe the arrangement honestly than overstate what we
need.

**Second, the status of CAP adoption.** We understand DHM held its first national CAP
training with WMO in April 2025 and that standard operating procedures were being
drafted. Our system emits CAP 1.2 natively. If we can see the SOP as it stands, we can
conform to it while it is still being written rather than retrofitting afterwards, and
we would be glad to contribute an open reference implementation if that is useful.

We would welcome a meeting with the flood forecasting section at their convenience.

Yours sincerely,

[SIGNATORY]

## Notes for the sender — remove before sending

- Blocker B2. The fallback is already designed in: the hydro detector takes a plain
  (times, stage) array, so a refusal delays fusion rather than stopping the project.
  Saying so in the letter costs nothing and makes the request easier to grant.
- Ask for *historical* series first. A real-time feed is a much larger request and
  asking for it now risks the whole thing.
- DHM's flood section runs on roughly five professional staff and six technicians.
  Keep any meeting short and specific, and do not arrive with a list.
- The CAP question is the cheaper half of this letter and may well be answered first.
  A narrow window is open while the SOP is still in draft.
