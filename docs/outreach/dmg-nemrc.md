# Letter — DMG / NEMRC

**To:** Director General, Department of Mines and Geology
**cc:** National Earthquake Monitoring and Research Centre
**Subject:** Collaboration on seismic detection of mass-movement events
**Status:** DRAFT — not sent

---

Dear [NAME],

We are writing about a use of your seismic network that we believe is currently
unexploited, and about which we would value your judgement before we go further.

On 26 August 2026 the ice-and-rock collapse that initiated the Bhote Koshi surge
produced a seismic signal large enough that USGS initially catalogued it as an M4.4
earthquake before identifying it as landslide-generated. That signal existed minutes
before the water reached the first settlements. Nobody was listening for it.

We have been examining whether such signals can be reliably distinguished from
earthquakes and from background noise on NK.KKN. Working from the openly available
archive, the separation is visible on the discriminants physics predicts: the 26 August
signal is markedly lower in frequency and longer in duration than the regional
earthquakes recorded on the same station in the preceding weeks. Whether that
separation survives contact with six months of continuous noise, and at what
false-alarm rate, is exactly what we do not yet know — and it is why we are writing to
the people who know this network best rather than proceeding alone.

Three specific requests:

**Station metadata and instrument responses** for NK stations, so that our features are
in physical units and comparable across stations rather than in raw counts. We are
currently working in counts, which limits us to a single station.

**Your view on real-time access.** We have measured latency on the public EarthScope
SeedLink relay and it appears adequate for our purposes. Before relying on it we would
like to know whether that relay is one NEMRC considers dependable, and whether a direct
arrangement would be more appropriate for anything operational.

**Collaboration, which is the substantive request.** We would like to build the labelled
event catalogue this work requires — Himalayan mass-movement events with usable seismic
records — together with NEMRC rather than in parallel. The catalogue is the scarce
asset here, more than any model, and it would be more useful to Nepal held by the
institution that operates the network.

We are glad to share our code, our data and our negative results, whatever comes of
this. We would welcome a technical discussion with your seismologists.

Yours sincerely,

## Notes for the sender — remove before sending

- Blocker B3. Least blocking of the three: archive FDSN access already covers M1–M3,
  and the preliminary latency result suggests the public relay may be sufficient on
  its own. This letter is about doing the work *with* NEMRC rather than adjacent to
  them, which matters more for the project's survival than the data does.
- Instrument response is issue 2.4 and is the concrete near-term ask.
- Be careful not to imply the network is being underused as a criticism. The framing
  is a use nobody has tried, not a failure by the people running it.
- Do not overstate the reconnaissance result. Four windows, one of each class, is
  not evidence of a detector, and this audience will know that immediately. Claiming
  more would cost the collaboration.
